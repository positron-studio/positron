## 1. What it is today

**ossia score** is a free, open source *"interactive sequencer for the intermedia arts"*.
It sequences OSC, MIDI, DMX, sound, video and more on a timeline, with interactive
triggers, conditions and branching. Timelines nest without limit and mix with a node graph.
[ossia.io](https://ossia.io/), [repo](https://github.com/ossia/score).

**libossia** is the library under it. A *"cross-environment distributed object model"*: an
application declares its parameters as a tree of OSC nodes (a "device") and exposes it over
the network, including OSCQuery so the tree can be explored. Bindings for Pd, Max, Python,
C, C++, openFrameworks, Unity, QML, Faust and SuperCollider.
[libossia README](https://github.com/ossia/libossia) (read).

### What 3.9.0 added (read, [release note](https://github.com/ossia/score/releases/tag/v3.9.0))

- A full 3D scene pipeline on the GPU: glTF, FBX, STL, point clouds, gaussian splats.
- AI models inside the graph: librediffusion (StreamV2V, Flux), ONNX pose detection,
  small vision language models, stem separation.
- Video capture cards, PipeWire video, depth cameras (Kinect, RealSense, Orbbec), OpenXR,
  PosiStageNet and TUIO tracking, CAN bus.
- A much larger JavaScript API and custom user interfaces built by script.
- A remote control: an HTTP server serving a WebAssembly remote page.
- A web build with camera and audio input, drag and drop media, documents opened from the
  page URL, zip project archives and local storage backups.
- "Custom apps": package a score as a branded app for the web and for Windows on ARM.
- A Windows ARM64 build.

### Platforms (read)

- macOS, Windows (x64 and ARM64), Linux (AppImage, Flatpak), FreeBSD.
- **Web**: a WebAssembly build at [ossia.io/score-web](https://ossia.io/score-web). It
  started as a Qt NaCl port in 2015 and was called *"very experimental"* earlier. 3.9.0
  treats it as a real target.
- **Raspberry Pi**: official ARMv7 and AArch64 builds, Pi 3 and 4 (4 recommended), Pi
  Zero 2 W reported to run. A headless player mode and a full screen EGLFS mode without a
  desktop. VST3 is off on ARM Linux. [embedded docs](https://ossia.io/score-docs/in-depth/embedded.html).
  Official Pi support dates from score 3 (Feb 2022, [post](https://ossia.io/posts/ossia-score-3/)).
- Taught at SIGGRAPH Labs 2025 as *"Real-Time Interactive Graphics on Embedded Systems with
  ossia score, Puara, and Raspberry Pi"*. [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3721251.3734069).

### Protocols (read)

OSC, OSCQuery, MIDI (including network MIDI), DMX (Art-Net, sACN, USB), HTTP, WebSocket,
serial, CoAP, MQTT, Bluetooth LE, gamepads, Wiimote, Leap Motion, NDI, Spout, Syphon, LTC,
Stream Deck, and protocol scripts written in QML. It is also sold on its own pages as a
protocol bridge and a DMX console. [protocol bridge](https://ossia.io/protocol-bridge.html),
[DMX](https://ossia.io/dmx-lighting.html).

### Scripting and code inside a score (read)

JavaScript (and a QML scripting layer), Faust, ISF shaders, C++ (the "avnd" API), PureData
patches, math expressions, JSFX, VST, VST3, LV2, CLAP plug-ins, plus generative languages
(Structure Synth, Context-Free Art, Bytebeat).

### Distributed execution (read, [post](https://ossia.io/posts/distributed/), June 2022, score 3.0.11)

- One machine does `File > Make server`, the others `File > Join`. Everything runs over
  WebSockets.
- The same session gives collaborative editing and distributed execution.
- **Groups** separate the score from the hardware. A Topology tab defines groups, and each
  object of the score is assigned to a group ("this part runs on computer A"). Groups are
  saved with the score, clients are not.
- Triggers can be evaluated on one machine and shared, or independently on each. Interval
  speed can be shared. A "Message Pit" process combines values from several clients (list,
  average, sum).
- Stated limits, in the post's own words: *"experimental"*, *"no warranty, no stability
  guarantees"*, and *"live temporal synchronization is currently very barebones"*. Media and
  VST paths must be identical on every machine.
- I found no newer post or doc page that updates this. A 2023 paper,
  [Networked Performances with Ossia Score](https://www.academia.edu/106859306/Networked_Performances_with_Ossia_Score),
  exists and was not read. **Whether the sync limit still holds in 3.9 is unknown.**

## 2. Who makes it and how it is sustained

### People

- **Jean-Michaël Celerier** is the lead developer. PhD at Université de Bordeaux under Myriam
  Desainte-Catherine (LaBRI, SCRIME), then a postdoc at Concordia in Montréal
  ([Hexagram workshop page, 2023](https://hexagram.ca/en/atelier-ossia-score/)). A LinkedIn
  result in search names him **Technology Development Director at the Société des arts
  technologiques (SAT), Montréal** (read from a search snippet only, not opened).
- **Pia Baltazar** coordinates the project. Two branches, France and Canada.
  [about](https://ossia.io/about.html).
- Lineage: Virage and i-score sequencers, then the ANR OSSIA research project (2012 to 2015),
  then a rewrite during Celerier's thesis. Blue Yeti, a multimedia company, co-funded through
  a CIFRE industrial PhD.

**Suite SAT's model** is the clearest price list in this picture
([suite.sat.qc.ca](https://suite.sat.qc.ca/en/)): a free Community tier (ossia score,
Puara, SpatGRIS, Domeport Web, Esquisse, all open source), Creator at **$19 CAD a month**,
Professional at **$99 CAD a month**, institutions **$700 CAD a month**, universities
**$7,500 CAD a year per room**, plus R&D, training and custom work by contact. A lapsed
subscription keeps the versions released while it was active.

Reading (inferred): the donation channels are tiny. ossia lives on research grants and on an
institution that sells training, rooms and the paid tools around a free core. The free core
is what makes the institution's offer credible.

### Licence (read)

## 3. How it is distributed and taught

- **Binaries** for every desktop platform, the Pi, the web, the Microsoft Store, Flatpak and
  AppImage; a Debian packaging request filed in 2024
  ([ITP](https://www.mail-archive.com/debian-devel@lists.debian.org/msg382650.html)).
- **Docs** at [ossia.io/score-docs](https://ossia.io/score-docs/), video tutorials, a Discord,
  a forum, a gallery, and a user library repo. 3.9.0 puts templates and online examples on the
  start screen.
- **Workshops**: free ones at Concordia and SAT's Art and Code meetup (2023), SIGGRAPH Labs
  2025. SAT sells personalised training.
- **Teaching**: Celerier teaches C++ and interactive music and mentors student groups on
  ossia projects ([portfolio](https://jcelerier.name/page/portfolio)).
- **Works and institutions** (read from the
  [Show HN, July 2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44492405), 95 points, and
  search results): two works using ossia-max at Ars Electronica 2025, one of them
  *Organism + Excitable Chaos* by Navid Navab and Garnet Willis, Golden Nica for Digital
  Musics and Sound Art; the Pavilion of Ireland at the Venice Biennale; museum installations;
  a light show running score headless on a Pi as a DMX console. Partner organisations named:
  SAT, GMEA, didascalie.net, iMAL, Les 7 Doigts, IDMIL.

an intermedia sequencer needs, and an institution that pays for it.

Honest summary (inferred): ossia is a mature instrument for building and running intermedia
works on machines you own. positron is a public notebook of small working pages, run on
someone else's edge, measured, and handed on as instructions. They overlap in patching,
timelines, devices and Pis. They differ in where a work runs (your LAN vs the internet), how
you enter (install vs a URL), and what travels (a binary vs a skill). Most of positron's
"differences" are choices, not capabilities ossia lacks: ossia has a web build too.

## 5. For the mentor conversation

### What ossia's path says about "code free, what is paid for"

- **Donations do not pay.** Thirteen years, a Golden Nica work, 2,000 stars: about $10k on
  Open Collective (one gift) and 2 GitHub sponsors. A free tool alone does not fund a person.
- **Institutions pay for the person.** Research grants, an industrial PhD, a MegaGrant, and now
  a job at SAT. The software is the reason the institution wants that person.
- **The paid layer is around the core, not the core.** SAT gives ossia away and charges for
  tiers of other tools, rooms, training and custom R&D. That is the recipe-and-cook pattern in
  `plan-techtrack-lab-2.md` section 4, already priced by someone.
- **Even the paid perks are about the person's time**: priority bug fixes, priority support,
  builds for your hardware.
- **Agents cut the code cost for the incumbent too.** ossia went from about 90 to about 700
  commits a month. "Code is cheap" does not favour a newcomer by itself.

### If a mentor asks "isn't this ossia?"

1. "Yes, in part. ossia score is the closest relative I found and I recommend it. It has done
   patching, timelines, devices and Raspberry Pis for thirteen years, better than I do."
2. "The difference is where the work lives. ossia runs on your machines and you install it.
   positron's pages run on the internet edge, open from a link, and talk to a Pi in another
   building. Each transport has a measured latency."
3. "The other difference is what I hand over. ossia hands you a program. I hand you
   instructions an agent follows to build your own, on your own account. ossia's own recent
   code is mostly written with an agent, but it does not ship that knowledge."
4. "ossia's history is also my answer about money. The code is free there too. What pays is
   grants, an institution and the person's time. I am asking you which of those fits me."
5. Do not claim the archive work as a difference until a page shows evidence kept apart from
   reconstruction. Say it is the direction.

### Worth asking or learning

- **Ask the mentors**: is the SAT pattern (free core, paid training, rooms, residencies,
  institutional subscriptions) reachable from Estonia, and who would play SAT's role here
  (EKA, BFM, an ERR archive project, a festival)?
- **Learn from ossia**: a headless Pi player and "custom apps" are how a work leaves the
  author. positron's equivalent is a skill plus a Cloudflare account. Worth stating as the
  same need answered differently.
- **Contact is plausible.** Celerier writes C++ with Claude daily now, and his project names
  Anthropic as a sponsor. A short note comparing "skills as distribution" with "binaries plus
  docs" is a real conversation, not a cold pitch. A practical bridge: positron could speak
  OSCQuery to a running ossia score, so the two meet rather than compete (inferred, not built).
- **Ask Celerier**: what made SAT adopt ossia, and what did the agent change in what he
  chooses to build.
